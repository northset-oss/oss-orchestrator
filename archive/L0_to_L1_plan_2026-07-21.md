---
The diagnosis: you're optimizing the wrong queue

Your instinct — "we're moving slowly at Level 0, so Level 1 is far away" — contains a false coupling. Level 1 is not downstream of Level 0 throughput. Your own campaign plan says so explicitly: the maintainer-requested verification lane is exempt from the authored social gates. It scales on demand, not on the age table. Shipping 10/day instead of 6/day does not move Level 1 one inch closer.

Level 1 is downstream of exactly two things, both fully in your control:

1. The boundary suite passing (engineering — blocks foreign-PR runs).
2. Individualized offers being made (outreach — nothing converts from infrastructure that sits there passively).

You have 20 merges and, as far as I can tell, roughly zero recorded offers. The bottleneck isn't reputation accumulation — it's that the ask has never been engineered as a first-class production line the way the receipt has. Your Level 0 factory is world-class; your Level 1 factory doesn't exist. Build it this week. Here's how.

Move 1 — The self-verification wedge: Level 1 is reachable this week, before the boundary suite

This is the highest-leverage insight in your own documents, and I don't think you've noticed it:

▎ "If a maintainer asks us to check a PR that we happened to author, that is still Level 1 — because the owner asked." (your Level 1 explainer, Step 2, Atom 2)

And the campaign gate reads: "Before foreign-PR run #1, the sacrificial-boundary acceptance suite passes." A Northset-authored PR head is not foreign code — it's the same code you already run in your authored lane every day.

Therefore: maintainer-requested verification of a Northset-authored PR is gate-legal today. The trigger flips (owner asks), the consent artifact exists, variant V, role verifier — the compass needle leaves zero. No boundary suite required.

Execution: in every PR thread where a maintainer is actively engaged (reviewing, asking questions, or at the "Approve and run workflows" friction point — your PRs come from a fork, so they must manually approve CI), add one contextual, non-promotional sentence:

▎ "If it helps your review: say the word (or add the northset-verify label) and I'll run your full check suite against this exact head in our isolated runner and attach a signed receipt — so you can see the checks passed before spending approval clicks on it."

That is one individualized message, warm, serving their review — squarely inside your outreach rules. You have 37 open PRs, each one a live channel. A handful of yeses here gets you your first 3–5 Level 1 runs within days, with real consent artifacts, while the boundary suite is still being built.

One honesty note: report these transparently as verification-of-own-authored-PRs (your per-type-with-denominators rule already forces this). Don't let all 10 gate runs be self-authored subjects — but as runs #1–4 they are legitimate, countable (MAINTAINER_AUTHORIZED is about who asked, not who authored), and they exercise the entire consent→run→private-delivery machinery on real external counterparties.

Move 2 — Timebox the boundary suite: it's a 14-item list, not a research project

The hostile-fixture suite is enumerated in your own plan: metadata access, env-var reading, credential discovery, docker.sock, host mounts, outbound HTTP, DNS exfiltration, process explosion, disk exhaustion, file-count exhaustion, output flooding, symlink escape, cross-job persistence, cross-job artifact access — plus the known archive-parsing bypass class from the adversarial review. Your isolation machinery (network-off phase, pinned images, throwaway containers) already exists from the authored lane.

This is roughly one focused engineering week. It is the only hard blocker between you and foreign-PR verification, and it's pure engineering — no external party involved, no waiting. Every week it isn't scheduled is a week you've chosen not to be at Level 1. Put it on the calendar as the top engineering priority, ahead of any Phase 1 yield-tuning that isn't itself gate-blocking.

Move 3 — The post-merge conversion playbook: turn 20 merges into 20 specific offers

The action plan already prescribes this ("merged PR → offer verification of another PR already in the maintainer's queue") but prescribes it as a principle. Make it a production step with tooling:

Build the "offer dossier" script (a day of work in oss-orchestrator): for each warm repo, pull the open PR queue and rank by verification pain — PR age, CI status, and especially fork PRs from first-time contributors stuck at "workflow awaiting approval" (the exact pain your game plan names, and one the maintainer feels every time they look at their queue). Output: repo → maintainer → the one specific PR to name in the offer.

Then, within 48h of every merge (while you're at peak goodwill), send one individualized message:

▎ "Thanks for the merge. I noticed PR #123 from a first-time contributor has been waiting 11 days with CI unapproved. If you'd like, I'll run your declared checks on it in an isolated container and send you the result privately — no public record unless you want one. One PR, one request, and 'stop' means stop."

This is warm, singular, names a real pain, and costs the maintainer one word to accept. (Foreign-PR offers go out only after Move 2 lands — but build the dossier tooling now so day one after the suite passes, 20 offers are ready to go.)

Run it as a funnel with numbers: offers made → yes → run delivered → five demand questions recorded → second invocation. Weekly target: 5 individualized offers. This is the metric that should headline your weekly review — not receipt count.

Move 4 — Rebalance Level 0 targeting: stop farming repos that can't ladder

Look at your merged list honestly: dreamstick9/filedrop, nandansai08/personal-ai, shamilahmdt/devtasks, chipmates/agoracosmica — personal side-projects. They inflate the merge count but their maintainers have no PR queue, no review debt, no money relationship. A merge there opens no door. Your own Level 0 doc says it: "The number that matters at Level 0 is not how many receipts exist. It is how many doors they open."

Change the factory's candidate scoring to weight Level-1 convertibility, not just fix-ability:

- Open community-PR queue depth (≥5 stale community PRs = review debt = verification pain)
- Fork-PR CI-approval friction observable in the queue
- Maintainer comments signaling drowning ("sorry for the delay," AI-volume complaints)
- Org-backed or multi-maintainer repos (KaotoIO, glideapps, meilisearch, objectionary, rokucommunity, ember-cli class — not solo hobby repos)
- Maintainer responsiveness (they merged/replied within days)

And farm depth over breadth: designate 8–12 "relationship accounts" and deliberately route repeat missions there (within your per-owner caps: ≤2 new PRs/day, ≤5 open). A second merge with the same maintainer is worth more toward Level 1 than a first merge with a stranger. Gate arithmetic makes this concrete: Step 2 exit needs ≥10 runs, ≥5 orgs, ≥3 second-run orgs — realistically you need 15–20 credible warm organizations to yield that. Today your warm org surface is maybe 8–10. Widen it by targeting, not by volume.

Move 5 — Flip Atom 1 early: let maintainers choose the mission

Level 1's defining atom is "the owner asked." You can start training that habit inside Level 0, with zero rule changes: with any engaged maintainer, ask —

▎ "We have capacity for one more fix here this week — which open issue hurts most?"

Their answer is an owner-initiated request. The mission stays a plain author_contribution under all existing rules, but the relationship has flipped: the maintainer has now asked Northset for something and gotten it. That's the asking muscle Level 1 needs, built one rep at a time — and maintainer-chosen issues will merge faster, too, because interest was pre-committed.

Move 6 — Harvest your rejections: the closed PRs are your best verification prospects

prometheus/client_js closed two of your three PRs. prettier closed one. nodejs/doc-kit requested changes. Your action plan already tells you what these are: "A maintainer concerned about AI-generated contribution volume → do not offer another authored patch → offer verification of their existing queue."

A maintainer who rejects authored AI patches but drowns in community PRs is the purest verification customer — they've told you they don't want more supply, which means their pain is triage. After the boundary suite passes, these get the verification-only offer, explicitly framed: "No more patches from us — but if it's useful, we'll run checks on what's already in your queue." Losing the merge but winning the verification relationship is a trade up.

Move 7 — Make the ask ambient, then exercise the machinery once so it's showable

- Run one full V-variant rehearsal on your own repo now. The verification machinery has never executed. Run it end-to-end (consent artifact, private delivery, V receipt), label it rehearsal, and publish the sample. Every offer in Moves 1, 3, and 6 can then link "here is exactly what you'd receive" — an artifact, not a description. Half a day; converts every future offer from abstract to concrete.
- Put the request affordance where maintainers already look: one line on every receipt page and in the org README — "Maintainers: request a private check of any PR — label northset-verify or this form." Your own site, non-promotional, zero marginal cost per mission.

Move 8 — Instrument demand now, including proto-signals

Stand up the ICP log and shadow-acceptance records from your 30-day plan (both are additive, no workflow change). One addition: log de facto delegation events. When a maintainer merges your PR without re-running CI themselves — visibly relying on your receipt — that's a proto-Level-1 signal: they let the check answer a question, they just didn't ask first. Those maintainers are your highest-probability first offers. You cannot see this pattern today because nobody is recording it.

What I would not do

- Don't accelerate Level 0 shipping to accelerate Level 1. The lanes are decoupled by your own plan's design. Phase 1 yield-tuning continues at its own honest pace.
- Don't batch or template outreach. One individualized message per relationship, no follow-up without engagement, ever — the account-survival strategy in your memory file and the campaign guardrails both depend on this.
- Never run anything without a recorded request — including "helpful demos" on someone else's PR. The consent gate is the product.
- Don't count contributor-requested runs as maintainer-authorized. Demand data, reported separately.
